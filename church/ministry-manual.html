School of Continuing Education • Leadership Manual

Holy Person & Plant Minister Leadership Training

A growing Earth-based and Indigenous-inspired ministry curriculum covering spiritual leadership, Medicine Wheel study, ecological literacy, ceremony education, youth learning, community services, ethics, supervised practice and continuing formation.

Scope and safety.

This is church education and spiritual formation—not a license to diagnose, prescribe, medically detoxify, provide psychiatric treatment or claim another nation's hereditary or community-appointed title. Medical withdrawal, suicidal thoughts, poisoning, severe heat illness and other emergencies require qualified real-world emergency or licensed healthcare. Nation-specific songs, languages, ceremonies and sacred protocols require source, permission and cultural context before the church presents them as traditional.

Foundation

The Wheel: plurality before universality

Medicine Wheel teachings vary among Indigenous nations and communities. Colors, directions, animals, seasons and meanings are not identical everywhere. Seven-Fold therefore teaches comparative structure while requiring students to identify whether a teaching comes from a particular nation/community, a documented source, family or practitioner knowledge, or an original Seven-Fold interpretation.

Sky & Seasonal Observation

Observe sunrise and sunset, solstices, equinoxes, daylight, seasons and local climate. Students compare these observations with documented cultural calendars without claiming one universal Indigenous system.

Life-Cycle Reflection

Study ways circular teachings can represent beginnings, youth, maturity, elderhood, transition, renewal and relationships among spiritual, emotional, physical and mental life.

Local Land

Students map local plants, animals, weather, soil and water and distinguish personal/ecological symbolism from teachings formally attributed to a people or lineage.

Path I • Holy Person Minister

Spiritual fire, ceremonial leadership & community care

Module 1 — Wheel, Seasons & Cosmology

Direction, seasonal observation, cycles of life, local ecology and comparative Medicine Wheel traditions.

Practicum: build a regional wheel with a source note for every culturally attributed element and clearly label original personal/ecological interpretations.

Module 2 — Holding Sacred Space

Circle facilitation, confidentiality limits, consent, listening, trauma-aware communication, de-escalation, prayer, silence, drum and sacred elements where appropriate.

Practicum: facilitate a supervised mock circle including opening boundaries, equal participation, difficult emotion and safe closure.

Module 3 — Life Rites

Wedding/partnership blessings, infant and child blessings, memorials, funerals, grief services and household/community blessings. Students learn preparation, family choice, symbolism and legal boundaries.

Module 4 — Prayer, Song & Sacred Sound

Church-created prayers are separated from nation-specific material. Indigenous-language songs or chants require verified language, source, community context and permission/public-use status before inclusion in training.

Four Directions prayer library:

The Academy may maintain original Seven-Fold prayers addressing Creator, Mother Earth, Father Sky, new beginnings, emotion, grounding, wisdom and kinship. These are labeled church-created unless a documented community source establishes otherwise.

Path II • Plant Minister

Ecological hand, botanical literacy & stewardship

Module 1 — Soil & Watersheds

Soil texture, sediment jar observation, organic matter, erosion, watershed mapping, runoff, land-use history and recognizing when laboratory or government environmental data is needed before making contamination claims.

Practicum: map the local watershed and identify likely pollution sources using reliable environmental records.

Module 2 — Botanical Identification

Taxonomy, field characteristics, seasonal forms, habitat, poisonous look-alikes, documentation and the rule that uncertain identification means no ingestion or medicinal use.

Practicum: build a supervised regional herbarium/photo record including look-alikes and source references.

Module 3 — Ethical Harvesting

Population health, permission, land ownership, protected species, sustainable harvest, seed production and culturally appropriate offerings. Fixed harvest percentages are studied as guidelines—not universal ecological laws.

Module 4 — Historical Plant Knowledge

Traditional, ethnobotanical and historical uses can be researched and taught with provenance. Plant Ministers do not turn historical uses into prescriptions or clinical treatment plans.

Clinical boundary:

Alcohol and sedative withdrawal can be life-threatening. A Plant Minister does not design herbal detox protocols for severe withdrawal, prescribe nerve-calming herbs, enforce therapeutic diets or replace medical detoxification. The ministry role can provide education, spiritual/community support and referrals alongside qualified healthcare.

Youth Curriculum

Children's Earth education

Listening to Soil

Jar-layer experiments, texture, moisture, roots, insects and the living soil community.

Meeting Plant Relatives

Drawing and photographing plants, learning conservation and look-alike safety. Children do not taste wild plants during lessons.

Following Water

Rain walks, runoff, pooling, streams, watersheds and clean-water stewardship with parent/guardian safeguards.

Intergenerational Assembly

Youth share soil charts, garden observations and water maps while elders, ministers and knowledgeable community members contribute stories and context.

Leadership & Governance

Separate responsibilities, shared accountability

Holy Person responsibility

Leads approved spiritual/liturgical practice and protects consent, dignity, participation boundaries and ceremonial order. Spiritual interpretation is not psychiatric or medical diagnosis.

Plant Minister responsibility

Can stop church use of a plant, water or material when identity or safety is uncertain. Safety concerns are documented and escalated for appropriate expert testing or review when necessary.

No unchecked authority

Neither role has unlimited personal power. Safeguarding, finances, complaints, credentialing and serious disciplinary matters require documented church governance and independent review rather than a single minister's decision.

Restorative response

Relapse is met without public shaming. Ministers provide spiritual/community support and help connect the person to appropriate medical, behavioral-health or emergency resources according to the situation.

Formation

Mentorship, supervised practice & assessment

Minister formation is designed as a substantial mentorship process rather than a quick online certificate. Final duration and numerical requirements are Church Board standards and can be strengthened as the Academy develops.

Shared Foundations

Church mission, ethics, consent, safeguarding, cultural provenance, research skills, crisis boundaries and AI literacy.

Specialized Study

Holy Person candidates deepen spiritual leadership and ceremony studies. Plant Minister candidates deepen botany, ecology, land and water studies.

Supervised Practicum

Observed teaching and facilitation, written field records, mentor feedback and demonstrated ability to stop when knowledge or authority is insufficient.

Continuing Education

Credentialed ministers continue study, source review, safety updates and periodic competency review.

Assessment

Leadership review

Spiritual Facilitation

Demonstrate calm, respectful circle leadership, boundaries, consent, source awareness and safe response to disruption.

Botanical Safety

Plant candidates demonstrate identification method and dangerous-look-alike awareness. A safety-critical misidentification requires retraining rather than being averaged into a passing score.

Crisis Scenario

If a participant reports suicidal intent, imminent danger or severe withdrawal, ministers stay supportive, do not shame or attempt an herbal substitute, and activate appropriate emergency/crisis and medical support while following safeguarding procedures.

Policy Development

Church charter principles

Future bylaws should protect inclusive membership, circle-based participation, transparent decision-making, cultural respect, sobriety-support without punishment, child safeguarding, privacy, financial accountability, complaints and appeals, conflicts of interest, credential standards, emergency procedures and clear limits on ministerial authority.

Governance distinction:

Spiritual leadership can be non-hierarchical without eliminating accountable governance. Consensus can be a preferred community method, while the legal governing body retains the duties required by the jurisdiction where the church or affiliate operates.
