Holy Person & Plant Minister Manual

A developing global ministry guide for spiritual leadership, Earth knowledge, ceremony study, community education and the collaborative responsibilities of Holy Person Ministers and Plant Ministers.

Educational and spiritual scope.

This manual supports church education, historical and cultural study, spiritual formation and ministry preparation. Holy Person Ministers, Plant Ministers and church AI do not diagnose disease, prescribe treatment or provide clinical detoxification. Medical emergencies and substance withdrawal require appropriate licensed healthcare. Tradition-specific ceremonies, songs, sacred objects and protocols must be taught and used with attention to their people, lineage, authority and community context.

Part I

Two ministry responsibilities

HOLY PERSON MINISTER

Spiritual & ceremonial leadership

Studies how prayer, sacred words, song, chant, drum, silence, fire, water, smoke, plants, sacred space and community participation can be responsibly brought together.

  • Prayer and spiritual leadership
  • Ceremony preparation and sequence
  • Song, chant, drum and sacred sound
  • Fire, water, smoke and plant elements
  • Life-cycle services and community circles
  • Consent, safeguarding and cultural responsibility
  • Knowing when an element should not be used
PLANT MINISTER

Earth, plant & ecological knowledge

Develops practical educational knowledge of the living environment and serves as a resource for members and ministry teams.

  • Plants, herbs, roots, trees and seeds
  • Identification and dangerous look-alikes
  • Soil, minerals, water and watersheds
  • Seasons, cultivation and seed saving
  • Responsible harvesting and preservation
  • Foodways, ethnobotany and historical uses
  • Ecology, conservation and safety research
Collaboration:

The roles are distinct but can work together. A Holy Person Minister may lead an approved spiritual service while a Plant Minister supports plant identification, sourcing, ecological knowledge and safety. A person may study both pathways when all requirements for each are completed.

Part II

Ceremony & purification studies

This curriculum studies purification-lodge traditions, sacred fire, heated stones, water/steam, prayer, herbs, sacred space, helpers and the spiritual symbolism of entering, cleansing and renewal. Students also study how protocols differ among peoples and why one community's ceremony should not be presented as a universal Indigenous ceremony.

Spiritual foundations

Earth, air, fire and water; renewal and rebirth symbolism; prayer; humility; kinship; relationship with Creator and the living world.

Roles & responsibility

Study of ceremonial leader, plant/ecological support, fire keeper/helper, participant care, consent, emergency planning and the limits of each role.

Materials & safety

Fire safety, appropriate stones, heat risk, ventilation, hydration, burns, smoke exposure, plant safety, accessibility and recognizing when a participant should not take part.

Tradition & provenance

Students identify the nation, community, teacher or documented source associated with specific terms, songs, sacred objects, directions and protocols before treating them as authorized practice.

Important:

The Academy will not teach a generic online recipe for conducting a sweat lodge. Detailed leadership of a high-heat ceremony requires qualified human instruction, local safety planning and appropriate cultural authority. The digital manual teaches context, responsibilities, preparation questions and safety boundaries.

Part III

Weekend services & community spiritual life

Holy Person service

Opening prayer or invocation, spiritual teaching, talking/listening circle, sacred music or drum where appropriate, reflection, community prayer and a closing blessing or weekly spiritual practice.

Plant Minister Earth report

Seasonal observations, garden and ecosystem conditions, water and soil awareness, a featured plant lesson, stewardship activity and safe educational resources.

Christian services & studies

Bible reading, prayer, sermon/reflection, pastoral care and Christian spiritual formation remain available as a full church path rather than being required of every member.

Digital participation

Online members can join appropriate study, prayer and educational gatherings while physical ceremonies remain subject to human leadership, location, safety and cultural requirements.

Part IV

Life-cycle ministry

Training includes weddings and partnership blessings, baby and child blessings, memorials and funerals, grief ministry, household/community blessings and other approved services. Students learn symbolism, preparation, consent, family preferences, cultural context and when legal or licensed professionals are required.

Safety and legal boundary:

Plant Ministers may educate about historical and cultural plant uses, but do not embalm bodies, prescribe herbs to infants or nursing parents, determine diets, or provide medical treatment under a ministry credential. Funeral care, marriage solemnization and other regulated activities must follow the laws of the place where the service occurs.

Part V

Prayer, chant, song & drum library

The Library will hold prayers, devotionals, sermons, songs and chants in separate source-aware collections. Material identified with a particular Indigenous language or nation must be checked for language accuracy, origin, permission and appropriate public use before it is taught as traditional.

Church-created prayers

Original prayers and reflections can be clearly labeled as Seven-Fold church material rather than attributed to a tribe.

Traditional material

Nation/community, language, source, teacher or publication, translation, usage context and public/restricted status are recorded where known.

Recovery & resilience

Spiritual encouragement can support people in recovery without promising that ceremony, sweating, herbs or prayer medically detoxifies the body or treats substance dependence.

Part VI

Youth Earth Education

Age-appropriate learning can introduce soil, plants, water, ecology, stewardship and spiritual respect for the Earth under parent/guardian safeguards.

Listening to Soil

Observe sand, silt, clay, organic matter, moisture, insects and roots. A jar-settling activity can introduce soil composition without treating dirt as lifeless material.

Meeting Plant Relatives

Drawing, photographing and identifying local plants; learning abundance, conservation and the rule that children never taste wild plants without a responsible adult who can positively identify them.

Following Water

Observe rain, runoff, pooling, streams and watersheds while learning why clean water and healthy soil support entire communities.

Intergenerational Learning

Elders, parents, ministers and knowledgeable community members can share stories, skills and observations while the Library preserves source and cultural context.

Part VII

Continuing formation

Students progress through shared foundations, specialized study, supervised practice, ethics and safety preparation, church review and continuing education. Completion of church training does not claim to confer a hereditary, nation-specific or community-appointed traditional title that belongs to another people's own process.
