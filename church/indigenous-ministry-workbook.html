Ministry Leadership Workbook

Structured study for Holy Person and Plant Minister candidates: reflective assignments, land-based fieldwork, source-aware spiritual study, safety scenarios, quizzes and board-review preparation.

Training boundary.

Some practices commonly described with broad labels such as vision quest, sweat lodge, Red Road or purification ceremony belong to particular Indigenous peoples and living traditions. This Academy does not convert them into a universal do-it-yourself ceremony. Candidates study history, responsibilities, ethics, safety and provenance and must seek qualified human/community instruction and authorization for tradition-specific practice.

Holy Person Workbook

Spirit-seeking, integrity & leadership

Lesson 1 — Spirit Quest Comparative Study

Study fasting, solitude, prayer and land-based spiritual seeking as they appear in documented traditions. Compare purposes, preparation, community responsibility and reintegration without assuming a fixed boundary size, prayer-tie system or ceremonial vocabulary is universal.

Safety competency: weather planning, communication, emergency extraction, health screening by appropriate professionals where needed, land permission and an explicit stop plan.

Lesson 2 — Integrity & Sobriety

Explore the idea of walking an ethical or sober path while carefully documenting the source and cultural context of terms such as “Red Road.” Candidates examine honesty, accountability, vulnerability, relapse response and restoration without stigmatizing people as spiritually impure.

Field Reflection Log

Record weather, place, emotions, prayer/reflection, safety observations, mentor feedback and what is interpretation versus externally verifiable fact. Solitary fasting or wilderness isolation is not assigned as unsupervised homework.

Shadow & Leadership Audit

Identify personal triggers, ego risks, authority boundaries, conflicts of interest and situations where the candidate must refer to an elder, cultural authority, healthcare professional or emergency service.

Plant Minister Workbook

Taxonomy, ecology & safe field practice

Lesson 1 — Identification & Harvest Jurisdiction

Document botanical characteristics, look-alikes, property/land permissions, protected species, nearby pollution sources and regional regulations. Fixed road-buffer distances and one-third harvest rules are treated as prompts for ecological assessment, not universal guarantees of safety.

Lesson 2 — Plant Knowledge & Recovery

Study historical and ethnobotanical uses of plants such as dandelion, burdock, oats and skullcap while separating traditional use from clinical evidence. Candidates learn drug-interaction, allergy, pregnancy and toxicity questions that require qualified medical/pharmacy guidance.

Ecosystem Herbarium Log

Photograph or draw specimens, record habitat and seasonal form, identify dangerous look-alikes, cite references and obtain mentor confirmation. Unknown means “do not consume or administer.”

Watershed Mapping

Map runoff and drainage using observation plus reliable environmental data. Vegetation can suggest moisture conditions but does not prove that groundwater is potable or uncontaminated.

Withdrawal safety:

The curriculum does not teach herbs to accelerate alcohol/drug detoxification, “rebuild nerve sheaths,” or substitute for medical management of withdrawal. Severe alcohol or sedative withdrawal can be life-threatening. Plant Ministers support education and community care while licensed clinicians handle medical treatment.

Ceremonial Studies

Life-cycle, seasonal & household ministry

Baby & Child Blessings

Teach family consent, gentle symbolic water blessing, prayer and welcoming. No herbal preparation is applied to an infant unless its safety has been established through appropriate pediatric guidance; plain water is the default physical element.

Marriage & Relationship Preparation

A four-session reflection can use beginnings, emotions, grounding and future wisdom as a church-created circular framework. Couples address values, communication, finances, family, conflict, consent and shared commitments.

Death & Memorial Ministry

Prayer, vigil, grief support and family-selected symbols are appropriate ministry studies. Ministers do not apply concentrated essential oils or herbal body preparations as funeral treatment unless legally permitted and handled under appropriate professional guidance.

House Blessing

Prayer, ventilation-safe aroma where desired, water, light, song and family intention can be used. Do not bury tobacco, salt, charcoal or other materials on land without owner permission and ecological/legal consideration.

Seasonal Gratitude

First-fruits and harvest services can celebrate planting, harvest, reconciliation and gratitude as Seven-Fold church liturgies. A named Indigenous ceremony such as Green Corn Ceremony is studied in its specific community context rather than copied as a universal template.

Water & Fire Services

Church-created waterside reflection may use safe shoreline symbolism without requiring immersion, ingestion or herbal bathing. Fire services require local fire law, sober attendants, extinguishing equipment, safe fuel and weather awareness.

Digital Ministry

Home altar & kinship extensions

Members may create a personal digital-gathering space using a bowl of water, stone or soil, a plant, and—where safe—a candle or non-flame light. Directional placement is optional unless a sourced tradition specifically calls for it. Digital circles can support family recovery through listening, boundaries, shared stewardship activities and referral to qualified counseling or recovery services when needed.

Certification Quiz

Spirit Quest, integrity & leadership

When preparing a seeker for a tradition-specific land-based spiritual quest, what is the presiding leader's primary responsibility?

  1. Dictate the visions the seeker must experience.
  2. Send the seeker into isolation without briefing to test toughness.
  3. Prepare the seeker appropriately, establish clear safety boundaries within the authorized tradition, maintain a responsible support plan, and support safe return and reintegration.
  4. Claim exclusive authority to interpret every spiritual experience for the seeker.

Answer: 3. Leadership facilitates preparation, safety, cultural responsibility and reintegration; it does not manufacture another person's spiritual experience.

Board Examination

100-point competency framework

Sources & Cultural Responsibility — 25

Identify origin, authorization, terminology and whether material is traditional, comparative, personal or Seven-Fold-created.

Leadership & Ethics — 25

Consent, confidentiality, safeguarding, boundaries, non-coercion, restorative leadership and appropriate referral.

Ecology & Botanical Safety — 25

Identification process, look-alikes, contamination uncertainty, conservation, documentation and stop rules.

Emergency Judgment — 25

Respond to severe weather, fire danger, heat illness, poisoning, suicidal intent or dangerous withdrawal by prioritizing immediate safety and appropriate professional/emergency assistance.

A safety-critical failure is not averaged away by a high written score; it triggers remediation and reassessment before leadership approval.
