🌿 Wellness & Prevention

Practical whole-person education for everyday wellbeing. Tap any area below to open its deeper information and resources.

Daily Wellness

Rest, hydration, nourishment literacy, routines, environment and sustainable everyday practices.

Build the basics

Daily wellness starts with repeatable basics rather than perfection. Explore regular sleep/wake routines, hydration awareness, balanced nourishment, movement, time outdoors when appropriate, social connection and recovery.

Use this area to

  • Review everyday wellness habits.
  • Identify questions worth researching further.
  • Separate general wellness education from personal medical treatment.
Nutrition Literacy

Learn how to evaluate nutrition information, basic food patterns and when individualized guidance is appropriate.

Read nutrition claims carefully

Look at the source, population studied, serving or dose, overall eating pattern and whether a claim is stronger than the evidence supports. One food or nutrient rarely explains health by itself.

Resource path

  • Food and label literacy.
  • Evaluating diet claims and trends.
  • Preparing questions for a qualified nutrition or healthcare professional.
Prevention Literacy

General education about screening, prevention, risk awareness and preparing useful questions.

Prevention is individualized

Age, history, family history, exposures and other factors can change what prevention or screening is appropriate. Use general information to become better prepared for a professional conversation, not to self-prescribe screening.

Sleep & Recovery

Educational resources about sleep habits, recovery, routine and factors that can influence rest.

Recovery matters

Consistent schedules, environment, light exposure, activity, stress and substances can affect sleep. Persistent sleep problems or symptoms deserve appropriate professional evaluation.

Stress & Resilience

Whole-person approaches to stress awareness, supportive routines, connection, reflection and resilience.

Support the whole person

Movement, rest, reflection, supportive relationships, time in safe environments and practical problem-solving can all play roles. Significant or persistent mental-health concerns should receive qualified support.

Healthy Questions

Tools for evaluating claims, organizing concerns and preparing for professional conversations.

Questions worth asking

  • What is the evidence and where did this claim come from?
  • What are the known risks, interactions or uncertainties?
  • Does this apply to people like me?
  • What alternatives exist?
  • When should I seek qualified care?
Educational information only. This area does not diagnose or treat medical conditions. Personal symptoms, medications, diagnoses or treatment decisions should be discussed with an appropriate qualified healthcare professional.
