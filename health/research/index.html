🧬 Research & Evidence

A health-literacy area for understanding what evidence says, how strong it is, where uncertainty remains and how to evaluate health information without turning research into unsupported medical claims.

Evidence Levels

Distinguish systematic reviews, controlled studies, observational evidence, case reports, hypotheses, traditional knowledge and personal experience.

Read a Study

Learn the basics of study design, sample size, controls, endpoints, statistical significance, limitations and conflicts of interest.

Source Verification

Trace important claims to primary or authoritative sources and check dates, authorship, methodology and corrections.

Emerging Research

Follow promising ideas while clearly labeling what is preliminary, uncertain or not yet established.

Traditional Knowledge + Research

Respect traditional and cultural knowledge without automatically presenting historical use as proof of clinical effectiveness.

AI-Assisted Research

Use AI to organize questions and locate material while verifying consequential claims against reliable sources and qualified expertise.

Research standard: evidence changes. This area should identify uncertainty and source quality rather than promise outcomes or substitute research summaries for individualized medical care.
